Ever feel like you’re trying to help your child with math, but it just leads to frustration? You’re not alone. As a teacher and a mom, I’ve seen the same well-intentioned mistakes pop up time and again. The good news is they’re easy to fix, and a small shift in approach can make math time something your child looks forward to.

One of the biggest missteps is starting with a worksheet. Think of it like serving broccoli before the pizza—it can kill the appetite for the whole meal. I learned this lesson vividly with my friend’s son, Liam. Eager to use our materials, she handed him a counting sheet first. He was done in three minutes, completely disengaged. The secret? Always start with the games. Our math packs are designed with play as the gateway. The games build natural curiosity and number sense, making the related worksheet activities feel like a fun extension, not a chore.

Another common error is using a one-size-fits-all activity. I made this mistake myself, giving the same puzzle to my four-year-old daughter and my nine-year-old nephew. My daughter was overwhelmed; my nephew was bored in seconds. Children develop at such different paces. The key is to choose activities that match your child’s current stage, not just their age. Our resources offer leveled variations so you can meet your child right where they are, building confidence instead of frustration.

We also tend to rush. Setting a ten-minute timer before dinner might seem efficient, but it often trains speed over understanding. True math confidence comes from stamina—the ability to stick with a tricky problem. Instead of racing against the clock, focus on depth. Let your child explore one activity thoroughly, even if it takes longer. Celebrate the thinking process, not just the quick answer.

When mistakes happen, our instinct is to jump in and correct them immediately. While coming from a place of help, this can make a child hesitant to try. It sends the message that being wrong is bad. Try pausing. Ask, “Can you walk me through how you got that?” Often, they’ll spot their own error. If not, you’ve gained insight into their thinking, which is far more valuable than a perfect worksheet.

Finally, math shouldn’t be a solo activity. When we make it a shared exploration—talking through a game, building patterns together—it becomes about connection and communication, not just correct answers. This collaborative spirit transforms math from a subject into a shared language of logic and fun.

So, what can you do today? First, lead with play. Open our math pack and play a game together before any pencil hits paper. Second, embrace the pause. When your child makes a mistake, take a breath and turn it into a conversation. Third, forget the timer. Focus on engaging with one concept deeply.

These simple shifts are how we build resilient, confident thinkers.
