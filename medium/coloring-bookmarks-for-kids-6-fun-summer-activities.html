Summer break is here, and if you’re like me, you’re already mentally preparing for the inevitable chorus of “I’m bored” by week two. But here’s a little secret that’s saved my sanity more than once: coloring bookmarks. They’re not just cute little slips of paper. They’re sneaky tools that boost fine motor skills, keep kids reading, and make road trips bearable. And the best part? You probably already have everything you need at home.

Let’s start with the motor skills piece. Those tiny hands need real work to build the strength they’ll need for writing in kindergarten and beyond. Coloring bookmarks require a controlled grip, careful pressure, and staying inside the lines—all of which naturally strengthen the small muscles in your child’s hands. It’s the kind of practice that feels like play, not a chore. Honestly, I’ve seen my own 6-year-old go from barely holding a crayon to confidently gripping a pencil, and it started with simple coloring projects like these.

Now, pair those bookmarks with summer reading. You don’t want your child losing all the progress they made during the school year, but forcing them to read can backfire. Here’s the trick: make the bookmark part of the adventure. When you color a bookmark together, that bookmark becomes a companion to the book. It’s no longer just an assignment—it’s a treasure map. Every time your child opens their book, they see their own creation marking the spot. That small ownership makes them want to keep reading, just to use their bookmark again. It’s a subtle shift, but it works.

Before you head to the craft store, let me save you some frustration. Not all paper is created equal for this activity. Standard printer paper? It bleeds. Markers soak right through, ruining the design on the other side and leaving a mess. Instead, grab some cardstock or heavy-weight paper. It holds up to markers, crayons, and even watercolors without warping. And if you want to get fancy, a laminator or clear contact paper will turn those bookmarks into durable keepsakes that last all summer long.

Finally, let’s talk road trips. Coloring bookmarks are the ultimate travel activity. They’re compact, mess-free, and keep little hands busy for miles. Pack a small pouch with pre-printed bookmark templates, a few crayons, and a clipboard, and you’ve got instant entertainment. My favorite trick? Print a set of themed bookmarks—ocean animals, space, or fairy tales—and let each child pick their favorite before you even leave the driveway. It turns the drive into a creative challenge: who can finish their bookmark before we reach the rest stop?
