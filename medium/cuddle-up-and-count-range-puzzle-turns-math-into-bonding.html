There are moments as a parent when you look over and see your child completely absorbed—not by a screen, but by something you placed in front of them. For me, that moment came with a simple printable called the Range Puzzle. My four-year-old niece Maya sat cross-legged on the floor, quiet and focused, sliding number pieces into place. When she finished, she turned around and gave her mom a high-five so enthusiastic it nearly knocked her over. That’s when I realized this wasn’t just another worksheet. This was connection disguised as math.

The Range Puzzle is a deceptively simple activity. It asks kids to place numbers or objects in the correct order within a given range, building their understanding of sequence and value. But the real magic happens when you sit down together. You might start with the puzzle printed out, but what follows is a shared experience—counting out loud, celebrating small wins, and watching their confidence grow. It turns a math exercise into a bonding ritual.

If you’re thinking, “That sounds great, but where do I even begin?”—I’ve been there. My son Evan grabbed a handful of gemstones meant for another game and scattered them everywhere during our first try. So here’s a practical tip: start small. Print just one row of the puzzle, not the whole page. Use objects you already have at home—buttons, LEGO bricks, or even snacks like crackers. Let your child touch and move each piece. That physical interaction is what makes the learning stick.

The beauty of this puzzle is that it grows with your child. For ages 4 to 6, skip the written numbers entirely. Use colored blocks or stickers that match sequences they already recognize from picture books. Let them position colors closest to the correct order. For older kids, ages 7 to 12, challenge them with larger ranges or timed rounds. One puzzle becomes a different experience every time you play.

Here are two actionable takeaways you can use tonight:

- Use a story hook. Before you start, say something like, “The number 5 is lost and needs to find its home between 4 and 6. Can you help it?” Kids love a mission.

- Add a physical twist. Instead of pointing, have your child hop from one number to the next on the floor. Movement locks in learning.
