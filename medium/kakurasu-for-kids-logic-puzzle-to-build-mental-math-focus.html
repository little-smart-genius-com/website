So what exactly is Kakurasu? Imagine a grid where each row and column has a target sum. Your child’s job is to shade the right squares so the numbers in each row and column add up to those targets. It sounds simple, but it’s incredibly satisfying. The puzzles start easy—perfect for ages 5 to 7—and gradually build in complexity, so older kids up to 10 get a real challenge. The best part? You don’t need anything fancy. No special trips to the store, no expensive apps. Just a printed worksheet, a pencil, and maybe a quiet corner. I keep a stack of our free Kakurasu printables in a drawer for those “I’m bored” moments. It’s a five-minute setup that buys you twenty minutes of focused, screen-free fun.

Here’s a tip that changed everything for us: start with a 4x4 grid. It’s small enough that kids don’t feel overwhelmed, but big enough to feel like a real puzzle. Show them how to check one row at a time, crossing off numbers as they go. If they get stuck, let them use small objects like buttons or coins to physically cover the squares. This hands-on approach works wonders for kids with ADHD or dyslexia, because it turns an abstract math problem into a tactile game.
