So how do you actually play the Word Pack with your child? It’s simple. Start by setting a relaxed, no-pressure tone. Let your child pick the words—whether they’re from their favorite book, a recent conversation, or something silly they made up. Then, take turns spelling them out loud, building them with letter tiles, or even drawing them. The key is to keep it fun and fast. If they miss one, no big deal. Laugh it off and try again. The goal isn’t perfection; it’s confidence.

One of my favorite tips from the full article is to weave vocabulary practice into your daily routine without your child even noticing. For example, Leo’s mom turned a simple “let’s do a word hunt tonight” into a weekend tradition. They’d hide word cards around the house, and Leo would race to find them, read them, and spell them back. It turned a quiet Saturday afternoon into a mini adventure, and he didn’t even realize he was learning.

Another actionable takeaway? Keep the boundaries clear but gentle. You don’t need to helicopter over their shoulder. Instead, guide them with a few simple rules: take turns, celebrate small wins, and stop before they get frustrated. When your child feels in control, they’re far more likely to stay engaged and even ask for more.
