# Coloring Bookmarks for Kids: 5 Mistakes Parents Make

*By David Moreau · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/coloring-bookmarks-for-kids-5-mistakes-parents-make.jpg)

I remember the first time I handed my daughter a beautiful, intricate bookmark with tiny mandala patterns. I was so proud of my choice. She took one look, pushed it aside, and grabbed a plain strip of paper instead. That moment taught me something I’ve seen play out again and again: **complexity kills creativity**, especially when it comes to coloring bookmarks for kids.

Here’s the truth most parents miss. We pick up those gorgeous designs with impossibly thin lines, thinking our child will shine. But what actually happens? Frustration. A half-colored mess. And a kid who decides coloring “isn’t for them.” The fix is simpler than you think. **Start with bold, simple shapes** — thick lines, large spaces, familiar objects. Your child’s confidence will skyrocket when they actually finish something.

And while we’re on the subject of finishing, let’s talk about the hovering. You know the scene. Your kid’s scribbling wildly outside the lines, and you gently say, “Try to stay inside, sweetie.” Stop. Seriously. **Coloring is about expression first, precision second.** That wild scribbling? It’s building arm strength and hand control your child will need later for writing. Every messy stroke is literally training their muscles for better handwriting. Let them be messy.

Here’s another mistake that’s easy to miss: **we default to crayons every single time.** Crayons are reliable, sure, but they don’t spark the same magic as other tools. Try swapping in **colored pencils for fine details** or **markers for bold, satisfying coverage**. You’ll be amazed at how a simple tool change reignites your child’s interest. The texture, the glide, the brightness — it all matters. Let them experiment.

Now, the big one. **Don’t turn coloring into a chore.** I see parents set timers or push for “five more minutes” when a kid is clearly done. That pressure kills the joy. Let your child decide when the bookmark is finished. Maybe it’s half-colored. Maybe it’s a scribble explosion. That’s okay. **The goal isn’t a perfect bookmark — it’s a happy kid who loves creating.**

Finally, here’s the mistake that breaks my heart the most: **forgetting to display the finished bookmarks.** I get it. Life is busy. But when you toss that bookmark into a drawer or recycle it, you’re telling your child their effort doesn’t matter. Instead, **put it in a book they’re reading. Frame it. Stick it on the fridge.** Seeing their work celebrated builds pride and motivation to keep going.

If you want your child to actually *use* those bookmarks — and build real skills in the process — you need to avoid these five common pitfalls.
