# Simple logic tables for children: Reasoning guide

*By Little Smart Genius · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/simple-logic-tables-for-children-reasoning-guide.jpg)

You know that moment when your child stares at a puzzle, then suddenly their eyes light up and they shout, "I got it!"? That’s the magic of logic tables. And honestly, most parents don’t realize they’re already using them. Those pattern blocks, sorting toys, or matching games on your shelf? They’re secret brain trainers. A simple logic table is just a grid where kids match, compare, or order things—like figuring out if the blue block is taller or if the big one comes first. There’s no game, no leaderboard, just pure, satisfying reasoning. For kids aged 4 to 12, this structure builds critical thinking without the overwhelm. It’s not about speed; it’s about seeing relationships.

The tricky part? Adapting logic tables for kids with ADHD, dyslexia, or autism. I’ve tried and failed at fitting a one-size-fits-all approach. Here’s what works: for a child with ADHD, ditch the worksheets entirely. Last year, a friend called me frustrated because her 8-year-old couldn’t sit still for two minutes with a fancy reasoning kit. We tweaked one thing: we made the table a physical game on the floor. He moved felt pieces and stickers around a cardboard grid. Suddenly, he was engaged for twenty minutes. For dyslexia, use high-contrast colors and large, clear symbols—skip the text. For autism, keep the rules simple and predictable. The same 2x2 grid can work for everyone, but you have to meet them where they are.

Now let’s get hands-on. You don’t need fancy supplies—just cardboard, markers, stickers, or felt pieces. Let your child help build the logic table from scratch. That’s where the real learning happens. Take Jordan, a 7-year-old with ADHD I met through a local playgroup. One rainy Wednesday, his dad spread out supplies on the dining table. Jordan chose stickers of dinosaurs and cars. Together, they made a grid: “Which one is faster? Which one is bigger?” Jordan wasn’t just solving puzzles—he was creating them. That ownership turned a simple activity into a confidence builder.

The beauty of logic tables is how they grow with your child. Start with a 2x2 grid and one attribute (size or color). Once they nail that, add a second row or a new category. You’re not just teaching reasoning; you’re building focus, flexibility, and a love for problem-solving. And the best part? You don’t need to be a teacher or a therapist. You just need a few minutes and a willingness to let your child lead.

If you’ve ever felt like your child’s learning style doesn’t fit the standard mold, this article is for you.
