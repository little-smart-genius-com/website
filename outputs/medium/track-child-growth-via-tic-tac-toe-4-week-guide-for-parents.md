Ever feel like you wish you could peek inside your child's growing mind to see how their thinking skills are developing? What if you had a simple, everyday tool that could do just that? Rachel Nguyen shares a brilliant, low-pressure method that turns the classic game of tic tac toe into a powerful window into your child's cognitive growth. It’s less about winning and more about witnessing the fascinating shifts in how they plan, solve problems, and even handle frustration.

**Why Tic Tac Toe is Your Secret Weapon for Tracking Progress**

Forget complicated assessments. As Rachel explains, tic tac toe acts like a stealthy brain scan. It’s a paper-and-pencil activity that reveals so much more than just who gets three in a row. When you know what to look for, a simple grid becomes a crystal-clear map of your child’s developing strategic thinking. Most parents see just a game, but you’ll start to notice the emergence of foresight, planning, and emotional control. That’s the real magic—transforming playtime into meaningful insight.

**Setting Up for Success and Knowing What to Watch For**

The beauty of this approach is its simplicity. Rachel emphasizes keeping it “stupid simple.” You don’t need a fancy setup, just consistency. Her pro tip? Don’t over-explain the rules at first. Simply say, “Let’s get three of our marks in a row,” and even talk through your own moves aloud to model thinking. “Hmm, if I go here, I see a chance to win.” This demonstrates strategy without pressure.

The core of the method is in the observation. It’s not just about playing; it’s about noticing. Over a focused 4-week period, you’ll play for just about 10 minutes a couple of times a week. During that time, you’ll watch for age-specific milestones. Is your 4-year-old starting to block your obvious wins? Is your 7-year-old setting up two potential winning paths at once? These tiny, brilliant shifts are the progress markers you’re tracking.

**Actionable Tips to Start Today**

You can begin applying this method immediately with a few key takeaways. First, **play the role of a commentator**. Verbalize your own thought process to give your child a model for strategic thinking. Second, **prioritize observation over correction**. Your main job is to watch and note their natural moves, not to coach them to win. Finally, **celebrate the thinking, not just the outcome**. Praise them when you see them pause to consider their options or when they successfully block your move, reinforcing the process.

To keep the engagement and progress soaring, Rachel’s full guide includes creative variations on the classic game. These twists on the rules gently stretch different cognitive muscles, preventing boredom and encouraging new types of problem-solving. It’s a fantastic way to adapt the challenge as their skills grow.
