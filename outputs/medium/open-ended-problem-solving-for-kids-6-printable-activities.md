# Open Ended Problem Solving for Kids: 6 Printable Activities

*By Little Smart Genius · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/open-ended-problem-solving-for-kids-6-printable-activities.jpg)

You know that sinking feeling when you hand your child a worksheet and they immediately groan, push it away, or declare, “I hate this”? I’ve been there. My own five-year-old once stapled a math page to the fridge just to avoid completing it. It’s frustrating, especially when you know they’re capable. But here’s the truth I’ve learned the hard way: it’s not about the answer. Open-ended problem solving for kids changes everything.

The magic isn’t in finding the “right” solution. It’s in the journey. When you swap rigid, single-answer worksheets for prompts that ask, “How many ways can you solve this?” or “What would happen if…?” something shifts. Reluctant learners become curious thinkers. I’ve seen it happen right at my kitchen table. Standard worksheets can kill curiosity, but open-ended prompts resurrect it. The key is giving children permission to explore, fail, and try again without judgment.

**Here’s what surprised me most: these activities build real resilience.** Parents often ask, “How can a printable piece of paper teach perseverance?” The trick is, it doesn’t — it’s what your child *does* with it that strengthens that muscle. The secret sauce is the space to struggle productively. When a child tries one approach, hits a dead end, and then pivots to another, they’re learning that setbacks aren’t failures. They’re just detours. That lesson sticks far longer than any memorized fact.

**One actionable tip I swear by: start with challenge cards.** One scenario per sheet, manageable and focused. No intimidating list of problems — just that quick win that builds momentum. For ages 8-12 especially, this format removes the overwhelm. Your child can tackle one open-ended question, take a break, and come back for another. It’s low-pressure, high-reward.

**Another tip: match the format to your child’s learning style.** Sometimes the reason for rejection is the format itself. Handing a craft-gifted child a fill-in-the-blank page is a setup for frustration. Visual kids need space to draw their thinking. Verbal kids need to talk it out first. Our Little Smart Genius printables offer multiple formats — drawing prompts, story-based scenarios, and hands-on challenges — so you can find what clicks for your unique learner.

**And here’s the best part: we’ve compiled six free, printable open-ended problem solving activities** that turn reluctant learners into curious explorers. No more groans. No more stapled worksheets. Just genuine engagement and those “aha!” moments that make all the effort worthwhile.
