# Cutting Strips for Kids' Safe Scissor Use: Summer Activities

*By Little Smart Genius · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/cutting-strips-for-kids-safe-scissor-use-summer-activities.jpg)

Summer break isn’t just about sunscreen and popsicles—it’s also when little hands lose their school-year strength. You know the drill: pencils disappear into couch cushions, scissors get forgotten, and by August, your child’s fine motor skills have taken a nosedive. But here’s the secret weapon you didn’t know you needed: cutting strips. These simple, themed paper strips are a quiet, low-mess way to keep those tiny fingers working while your kid thinks they’re just having fun. And honestly, that’s the best kind of summer activity.

**Why cutting strips are a fine motor power move**

Think of scissor practice as a workout for your child’s hand muscles. Every open-and-close motion strengthens the small muscles that control pencil grip, buttoning, and even tying shoes. But here’s the kicker—most kids resist formal drills. Cutting strips solve that problem by turning practice into play. Instead of a boring worksheet, you hand them a strip of paper with a zigzag line leading to a friendly ice cream cone. Suddenly, they’re motivated to cut carefully because they *want* to reach that picture. The repetition builds confidence without the frustration. One mom I know started using these with her kindergartner last June, and by July, she was cutting straight lines without tearing the paper. That’s the kind of progress you can actually see.

**Five summer themes that make scissor practice irresistible**

You don’t need to reinvent the wheel. Just match the cutting strips to what your child already loves about summer. Try a beach theme with wavy lines that mimic ocean waves, leading to a sandcastle or a seashell. Or go with a garden theme where they cut along a path to reach a sunflower. For the bug-obsessed kid, a “butterfly trail” with curved lines works wonders. Picnic-themed strips with straight lines leading to a watermelon slice are perfect for beginners. And if your child is ready for a challenge, try a camping theme with zigzag lines pointing toward a tent or a campfire. Each strip builds a different scissor skill—straight lines for control, curves for turning, zigzags for precision—all while sneaking in vocabulary and cognitive skills like sequencing and following directions.

**Actionable tips to try this week**

Start with thick, high-contrast lines that are easy to see. Print the strips on sturdy paper so they don’t crumple mid-cut. And here’s a pro tip: let your child choose the theme. When they pick the beach or the garden, they’re more invested in the outcome. Also, keep sessions short—five to ten minutes is plenty. The goal is mastery, not burnout. If your child struggles, offer a thicker line or a smaller pair of scissors. Celebrate every snip, even if the line looks like a roller coaster. Confidence is the real prize.
