# Counting Exercises for Preschoolers: Ultimate Guide

*By Lina Bautista · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/counting-exercises-for-preschoolers-ultimate-guide.jpg)

I used to think any worksheet would do the trick. Then I watched a five-year-old named Emma prove me wrong in about twenty seconds flat. She grabbed a counting sheet I’d handed her—plain black text, rows of numbers in 18-point font—and her eyes glazed over before she even picked up a crayon. Twenty seconds. That was it. And honestly? That moment changed everything I thought I knew about teaching preschoolers to count.

Here’s the secret most people miss: **design matters more than the numbers themselves.** A bright yellow sheet with a cheerful character, a simple layout, and just enough white space? That same child who couldn’t last half a minute suddenly sat there circling, counting, and asking for “just one more.” The magic isn’t in the math—it’s in how you present it. When you use printable worksheets designed with purpose, you’re not just handing out busywork. You’re creating a moment where learning actually sticks.

But let’s be real for a second. How many of us have printed a stack of worksheets, handed them over with high hopes, and then watched little Johnny start redecorating the furniture by minute four? I’ve been there. You’ve been there. The problem isn’t your child—it’s the approach. **Starting strong means starting small.** Pick one exercise, not ten. Sit with them, point to the first object, and say the number out loud together. Build that tiny win, then add another. Consistency beats intensity every single time.

Now here’s where it gets really fun. What if your preschooler won’t sit still at all? Meet Mia, a six-year-old who learned to count red cars from the back seat of her mum’s car. She had a printable number hunt on her lap—a simple sheet with pictures of cars, trucks, and buses—and every time she spotted a red car, she circled it and said the number out loud. **Learning on the move is not only possible, it’s powerful.** Kids who wiggle, bounce, and fidget often absorb information better when their bodies are engaged. A printable that travels with you turns a car ride, a waiting room visit, or a rainy afternoon into a counting adventure.

You don’t need a curriculum. You don’t need a degree in early childhood education. What you need is one smart worksheet, a curious child, and a willingness to try something different. The full article breaks down exactly how to choose the right counting exercises, how to use them in your homeschool routine, and how to keep even the most active little learner engaged—without losing your mind.
