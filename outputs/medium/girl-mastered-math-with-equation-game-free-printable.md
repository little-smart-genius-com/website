# Girl Mastered Math with Equation Game (Free Printable)

*By Sarah Mitchell · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/girl-mastered-math-with-equation-game-free-printable.jpg)

If you’ve ever watched your child push away a math worksheet with a sigh or, worse, slide under the table to avoid it, you know the feeling. I’ve been there too. One of my students, Lila, was convinced math wasn’t for her. She’d hide during number time, and it broke my heart. But here’s what I discovered: sometimes the problem isn’t the math itself—it’s the way we present it. The moment I swapped worksheets for a simple, playful game, everything changed.

That game is called **Kids Math Equations**, and it’s built around a tiny dry-erase board covered in scribbled equations. In the center sits a golden circle for the answer. The goal? Clear the board by solving each equation correctly and erasing the box. Sounds simple, right? But underneath that simplicity, something powerful is brewing. While kids race to erase boxes, they’re actually building **number fluency, working memory, and pattern recognition**—all without realizing they’re learning. They’re practicing focus, patience, and the quiet confidence that comes from solving problems on their own.

Here’s the step-by-step magic: hand your child the board, let them pick any equation to start, solve it, and erase that box. The board clears one square at a time. It’s low-pressure, hands-on, and oddly satisfying. I watched a nine-year-old named Sam stare at the board like it was a foreign language. Then he zeroed in on *2 + 3 =*, filled in 5, and erased the box. That was the spark. Within ten minutes, he had cleared fifteen boxes. No tears. No hiding. Just triumph.

To keep the excitement fresh, try these six creative variations: race against a timer, play with a partner taking turns, or use the board as a cooperative challenge where you solve equations together. You can even turn it into a memory game—solve one equation, flip the board, and recall the answer later. Each variation targets a different skill, from speed to teamwork to recall.

One thing I always remind parents: the real secret isn’t the equations themselves. It’s the **hidden confidence** kids build when they erase a box after solving it. That small act of clearing the board tells their brain, “I did it. I can do this.” And that feeling sticks.
