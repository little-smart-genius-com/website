# Thanksgiving Travel: Boosting Fine Motor Skills On-the-Go

*By Little Smart Genius · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/thanksgiving-travel-boosting-fine-motor-skills-on-the-go.jpg)

If your child can sit still for a tablet game but loses interest in a coloring page after five minutes, you’re not alone. I’ve been there, too. But here’s the surprising truth I’ve learned from countless car rides, airport waits, and restaurant tables: a simple coloring page can actually hold a child’s attention longer than any screen-based app. The trick isn’t the page itself — it’s how you use it.

When you’re packing for Thanksgiving travel, it’s tempting to grab a handful of crayons and a coloring book. But after about thirty minutes of what looked good on Pinterest but felt flat in reality, my kids started complaining. That’s when I realized we needed more than just paper and color. We needed a system.

Let’s be real: traveling with kids is chaos. Snacks spill. Seatbelts tangle. And somewhere around mile 45, the whining begins. My 5-year-old, Leo, used to get restless on our 3-hour drive to grandma’s house. But once I started using coloring pages with a purpose — not just as a time-filler, but as a fine motor skill booster — everything changed.

**Why coloring pages work better than screens** — It’s not about the activity itself. It’s about the sensory experience. When kids hold a crayon, they’re strengthening the small muscles in their hands and fingers. That’s the same muscle group they’ll use for writing, buttoning, and zipping. Screens don’t offer that. Plus, coloring pages give kids a quiet, focused task that doesn’t overstimulate them. That means fewer meltdowns and more peaceful travel time for you.

**Creative variations to keep kids engaged** — Tired of the same old coloring routine? I sure was. Here’s what saved our last trip: I bought a pack of blank label stickers and wrote numbers 1 through 6 on each of them. Then I set up a simple color-by-number system using our Little Smart Genius Thanksgiving coloring pages. My oldest stayed quiet for twenty solid minutes on a two-hour drive. Another trick? Cut the page into puzzle pieces before you hand it over. Your child has to color each piece, then put it back together. It’s like two activities in one.

**Common mistakes parents make** — The biggest one is overpacking supplies. You don’t need a full art kit. A few crayons, one or two pages, and a clipboard are plenty. Too many options overwhelm kids and lead to mess. Another mistake is handing over the page without any direction. Instead, give a simple challenge: “Can you color this turkey using only warm colors?” or “Let’s see if you can stay inside the lines for the whole feather.” That small twist turns a passive activity into an active learning moment.

If you’re heading to grandma’s this Thanksgiving, skip the tablet. Grab a set of our Little Smart Genius Thanksgiving coloring pages, a handful of crayons, and one of these creative twists.
