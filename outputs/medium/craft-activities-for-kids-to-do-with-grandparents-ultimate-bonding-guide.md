# Craft activities for kids to do with grandparents: Ultimate bonding guide

*By Rachel Nguyen · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/craft-activities-for-kids-to-do-with-grandparents-ultimate-bonding-guide.jpg)

There’s a secret to making craft activities for kids to do with grandparents actually stick. It’s not about the perfect supplies or the fanciest instructions. It’s about turning the whole thing into a game. My neighbor Deb, who’s 68, figured this out by accident. She told me her grandkids used to lose interest after five minutes of coloring. Then she started keeping a simple scorecard. Suddenly, they were begging to do more crafts. That’s the magic of a challenge system.

The problem with most craft time is that it feels flat. You get bored. Your kid gets bored. Even grandma gets bored. But let a seven-year-old pull ahead on a chart, and you see real focus. The idea is simple: every craft the pair completes earns points toward a reward. It could be a special treat, a sticker, or even just bragging rights. The act of tracking progress turns a quiet afternoon into a fun competition.

Here’s what I’ve learned about making these activities work across different ages. For kids under five, keep it big and forgiving. Use pre-cut strips of paper and wider tape. Their little hands need room to succeed. For older kids, add smaller steps and more complex building. The process teaches patience and fine motor control without feeling like a lesson. When my own mother started doing weekly craft sessions with her grandkids, she noticed the older ones would race to finish first, while the younger ones just wanted to make a mess. Both are valid.

**One actionable tip:** Use a simple point system for each completed step. Folding a paper airplane? That’s one point. Decorating it? That’s two more. Reaching ten points unlocks a small prize. This keeps kids engaged and grandparents involved without pressure.

**Another tip:** Let the child lead the design. Grandparents often want to help too much. Instead, ask the child to explain their idea first. This builds confidence and communication skills. The grandparent’s job becomes asking questions like, “What color should we use next?” or “How do we make this part stay together?”

The real payoff isn’t just a finished craft. It’s the voice that stays with your child long after the session ends. I still remember my ten minutes of folding paper chains with my grandpa. His voice telling me, “That fold works – now try it bigger!” stuck with me more than any worksheet ever could. You create shared memories that build cognitive skills naturally.

**Third tip:** Make the challenge visible. Hang a chart on the fridge or use a whiteboard. Each completed activity gets a checkmark. Kids love seeing their progress. Grandparents love the bragging rights. It turns a one-time activity into an ongoing tradition.

You’ll find specific craft ideas for every age group, plus tips for keeping the momentum going week after week. No complicated instructions. No expensive supplies. Just a simple system that makes bonding feel like play.
