# Track Your Child's 6-Week Learning Milestones with Free Pack

*By David Moreau · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/track-your-childs-6-week-learning-milestones-with-free-pack.jpg)

You know that feeling when you’ve printed a stack of activities, your child finishes them in ten minutes, and you’re left wondering: *Did that actually teach anything?* Most freebies fail us on this exact front. They’re fun for an afternoon, then forgotten. But what if you could see real, measurable growth over six weeks—without turning your kitchen table into a classroom?

That’s exactly what this progress tracking approach delivers. Think of it like a fitness tracker, but for your child’s brain. The pedagogical goal here is simple: transform those one-off tasks into a cohesive six-week story of skill-building. You’re not just keeping them busy. You’re collecting tangible proof that they’re advancing. No guesswork, no “I think they’re getting it.”

The best part? You only need three things to make this work. First, the full freebie pack (grab it below—it’s your main toolkit). Second, a simple folder or binder to keep everything organized. Third, about fifteen minutes of prep time once a week. Sarah, a mom to five-year-old Leo, swears by this Sunday evening ritual. She’d paperclip each week’s activities together, and by Monday morning, everything was ready to go. That small habit saved her from scrambling mid-week.

Now, here’s where it gets exciting. This isn’t just a set of worksheets—it’s a built-in tracking system. As you move through each week, you’ll see clear, visual proof of growth. Your child might start week one struggling to trace a shape, and by week four, they’re drawing it freehand. You’ll know exactly where they are, and more importantly, where they’re headed.

But what about when things get tough? I learned this the hard way. During week three, nine-year-old Ben in my class was ready to give up on spatial reasoning puzzles. He threw his hands up. “I can’t do it!” That’s when we adjusted—scaled back the difficulty, celebrated one small win, and built back up. The tracking system caught the struggle early, so we could pivot instead of push through frustration. That’s the real power here: you’re not locked into a rigid plan. You’re following your child’s actual pace.
