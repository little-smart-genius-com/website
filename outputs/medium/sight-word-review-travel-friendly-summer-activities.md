# Sight Word Review: Travel-Friendly Summer Activities

*By Sarah Mitchell · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/sight-word-review-travel-friendly-summer-activities.jpg)

Summer break is here, and the suitcases are packed. But somewhere between the sunscreen and the snacks, there’s a nagging worry every parent knows: *will my child forget everything they learned?* The so-called summer slide is real, and it can steal two to three months of hard-won reading progress. But here’s the secret I’ve learned from years of traveling with a neurospicy family — you don’t need a classroom-in-a-bag to keep learning alive. You just need the right tools, a little creativity, and a plan that actually works when you’re stuck in a car, a waiting room, or 30,000 feet in the air.

Let’s start with the biggest travel headache: airplane travel. It’s loud, cramped, and the moment your child gets bored, the whole cabin knows it. But here’s the twist — that same high-energy, restless moment is actually a golden opportunity for sight word review. I’ve learned this the hard way, but now I pack our **Little Smart Genius sight word review worksheets** like a secret weapon. They’re compact, portable, and designed for short bursts of focus. We play “word guard” — my child holds the worksheet and “guards” each word as we read it together. It’s a game, not a chore, and it turns a potential meltdown into a proud moment of “I know that word!” The trick is to keep sessions short — just five minutes at a time — and let your child feel like the leader.

What about long car rides? This is where the magic really happens. Instead of shoving a stack of papers at your kid and hoping for the best, try this: **match the difficulty to their mood.** I can’t tell you how often I’ve seen a parent hand a child a worksheet that’s way too hard, expecting joy, only to get an explosion instead. The honestly better approach is to use our **age-appropriate summer practice worksheets** that come in clear, simple levels. Start with bold, large words for beginners, and move to smaller text and quicker writing frames as they gain confidence. The key is choice — let your child pick which page they want to do. When they feel in control, the resistance melts away.

Here’s a specific tip that works like a charm: turn the worksheet into a scavenger hunt. Before you leave, print a few pages of our **travel-friendly sight word review sheets**. On the road, say “Find the word ‘and’ on your page, then find it on a road sign!” This builds real-world connections and keeps their brain engaged without feeling like schoolwork. For plane trips, use the same sheets but add a timer — “How many words can you read before the seatbelt sign turns off?” It’s playful, low-pressure, and effective.

Because summer travel doesn’t have to mean a learning break. It can be the very thing that makes sight words stick — without the tears, without the struggle, and with plenty of room for ice cream.
