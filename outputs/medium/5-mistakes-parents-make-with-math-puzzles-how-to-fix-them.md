# 5 Mistakes Parents Make with Math Puzzles & How to Fix Them

*By David Moreau · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/5-mistakes-parents-make-with-math-puzzles-how-to-fix-them.jpg)

You know that moment when you hand your child a math worksheet, and they look at you like you just asked them to clean their room for the hundredth time? I’ve been there. My own daughter once winged her pencil across the room when she saw a page of numbers staring back at her. And honestly? I don’t blame her. Most math worksheets are boring. That’s not a parenting failure — it’s a design problem. But here’s the good news: you can fix it without turning your kitchen table into a battleground.

The biggest shift I made? I stopped treating math like a race. Kids are not racehorses. When you say “hurry up,” their brain freezes. What actually works is rhythm, not speed. Set the numbers in a way that feels like a game, not a test. No timing. Just connection. That one change turned tears into giggles for my niece — and it can do the same for your child.

I’ve rounded up the five most common mistakes parents make with math puzzles, and more importantly, how to fix them. Mistake number one? Most parents skip the setup. They treat printable math packs like worksheets — scribble, finish, grade, done. But that kills all the fun before it even starts. Instead, try this: before you hand over the page, sit down with your child and explore it together. Point out the patterns. Ask them what they see. That simple shift turns a chore into a shared adventure.

Here are two actionable fixes you can try today. First, ditch the timer. Instead of racing through ten problems, aim for five done with confidence and a high-five. Second, let your child be the teacher. Have them explain their thinking out loud. You’ll be amazed at how their confidence grows when they feel like the expert in the room.

The full article walks you through all five mistakes step-by-step, plus gives you a proven system for frustration-free fun that keeps the magic alive even after a hundred sheets. No more tears. No more pencil-tossing. Just real connection and genuine math confidence.
