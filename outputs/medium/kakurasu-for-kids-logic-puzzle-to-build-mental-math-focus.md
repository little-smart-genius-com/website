# Kakurasu for Kids: Logic Puzzle to Build Mental Math & Focus

*By Little Smart Genius · Little Smart Genius*

---

![Featured Image](https://littlesmartgenius.com/images/og/kakurasu-for-kids-logic-puzzle-to-build-mental-math-focus.jpg)

Picture this: a quiet afternoon, a snack plate of orange slices, and your child completely absorbed in a grid of numbers. Not a screen in sight. That’s the magic of Kakurasu—a logic puzzle that feels like a game but secretly builds mental math, focus, and problem-solving skills. I know, it sounds too good to be true. But I discovered it two years ago on a rainy day with my then-five-year-old daughter, June, and I was a skeptic too. She wasn’t a math whiz. She’d rather draw unicorns than count. But when we printed a free Kakurasu sheet from Little Smart Genius, something clicked. She was adding rows and columns without realizing it, just to figure out which squares to shade. That’s the secret sauce: kids think they’re cracking a code, but they’re actually practicing addition, logic, and patience—all at once.

So what exactly is Kakurasu? Imagine a grid where each row and column has a target sum. Your child’s job is to shade the right squares so the numbers in each row and column add up to those targets. It sounds simple, but it’s incredibly satisfying. The puzzles start easy—perfect for ages 5 to 7—and gradually build in complexity, so older kids up to 10 get a real challenge. The best part? **You don’t need anything fancy.** No special trips to the store, no expensive apps. Just a printed worksheet, a pencil, and maybe a quiet corner. I keep a stack of our free Kakurasu printables in a drawer for those “I’m bored” moments. It’s a five-minute setup that buys you twenty minutes of focused, screen-free fun.

Here’s a tip that changed everything for us: **start with a 4x4 grid.** It’s small enough that kids don’t feel overwhelmed, but big enough to feel like a real puzzle. Show them how to check one row at a time, crossing off numbers as they go. If they get stuck, let them use small objects like buttons or coins to physically cover the squares. This hands-on approach works wonders for kids with ADHD or dyslexia, because it turns an abstract math problem into a tactile game. Our Little Smart Genius worksheets include **neurodiversity-friendly adaptations** specifically for that reason—larger grids, clear fonts, and optional color-coding for visual learners. You can even pair it with a snack break, just like we did with those orange slices. The combination of movement, touch, and reward keeps their brain engaged.

Ready for a daily adventure? **Start with our free Kakurasu printable** from Little Smart Genius. It’s designed to be a five-minute brain warm-up—perfect for before homework or after dinner. The key is consistency, not perfection. Do one puzzle a day for a week, and you’ll notice a shift: your child will start adding faster, checking their work more carefully, and even asking for harder puzzles. That’s the kind of quiet confidence that builds real focus.
