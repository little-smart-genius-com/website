Frequently Asked Questions

How often should my child do spot the difference puzzles for best results?

Aim for 2-3 times per week, 10-15 minutes each session. Consistency matters more than duration. Use our progress chart to track improvements over 4-6 weeks.

Can spot the difference puzzles really improve reading skills?

Yes. Visual discrimination is a key predictor of reading fluency. By training the brain to notice tiny details, children strengthen the same neural pathways used for letter and word recognition.

My child gets frustrated quickly. What should I do?

Start with easier puzzles (3-5 differences) and gradually increase difficulty. Praise effort over speed. If frustration peaks, take a break and return later. The goal is confidence, not stress.

Are photorealistic puzzles better than cartoon ones for visual memory?

Research suggests photorealistic images create stronger memory traces because they match real-world objects your child encounters daily, leading to better retention and transfer of skills.

How can I measure progress without formal tests?

Use a simple log: note time taken, number of differences found, and any comments from your child. Watch for trends like faster completion, fewer hints needed, and increased enthusiasm.
